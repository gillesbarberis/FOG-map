# Preserving Spatial Intent
## Rehearsing the Afterlife of a Sound Work

**Gilles Barberis · Research proposal · Updated 24 September 2026**

This is a proposed new artistic case study, developed for an EMAP residency application. The composition, reconstruction exercise and documentation framework have not yet been realised or validated. No residency, named collaborator or institutional research partnership is confirmed.

## The question

What must be transmitted, and what may change, for another practitioner to re-perform the spatial relationships of this particular sound work?

The proposal brings electronic composition into dialogue with nearly two decades of film-sound restoration. It does not seek to invent a universal converter, a new interchange standard or a claim of identical perception across systems.

## An artwork that rehearses its own future

An approximately 12–15-minute multichannel composition would be developed with a collaborating artist. Its material would draw on concrete and field recordings, synthesis, irregular pulses, physical resonance and ritual repetition. Separation and convergence, proximity and withdrawal, and the relation between a focused presence and an enveloping field are possible compositional starting points, not a prescribed catalogue of effects.

During creation, the artists would assemble a re-performance dossier: source assets, a spatial score, implementation notes, dependencies and explicit decisions about essential relationships and permitted adaptations. The original session would be safely retained but withheld from one reconstruction exercise. The collaborator would interpret an alternative realisation from the agreed documentation and assets.

The first comparison would change either the rendering method or the loudspeaker configuration. A layout adaptation would not be described as proof of cross-renderer interoperability. Because the collaborator would already know the work, the reconstruction would not be called a blind test.

## Listening conditions belong in the documentation

Futures of Listening is relevant here as a research reference, not an affiliation. Its programme connects spatial sound with collective listening, perception, accessible systems and new instruments. For this proposal, that suggests documenting not only sound files and spatial control, but also the arrangement and experience of listening.

The dossier would therefore record listening positions, permitted movement, audience arrangement and presentation instructions. Small voluntary sessions, with seated participation available, would compare selected passages. Technical checks would establish whether assets, routing and timing function; discussion would address whether the agreed relationships remain legible. Different responses would be retained rather than averaged into an ideal listener. This is qualitative artistic inquiry, not statistical validation of perceptual equivalence.

## Scope and outputs

The intended eight-week outcome is a finished sound work, one documented alternative realisation, and an openly reusable documentation template. A quiet room and a suitable multichannel setup are the baseline. Four planar channels are a feasible starting point for a deliberately bounded piece; six to eight offer more flexibility. Height channels and access to particular proprietary systems are optional. Licences, routing and technical support would be agreed with the host.

TENS may contribute recorded material or an optional performer-controlled input. Completing a spatial controller is not a requirement of the proposed residency. Existing artistic relationships, including remix work with Arsène Martinet, do not imply a commitment to this research project.
